Why is BC transit so low (0.008)? TransLink runs largely on electricity from BC Hydro, which is predominantly hydroelectric. US transit systems mostly use diesel or mixed-grid electricity, hence the higher factor.
Note on BC long-distance train (0.008): This is a deliberate methodological choice — we apply BC's electric rail factor to train travel to BC events. In reality, not all BC rail is electric (VIA Rail and Rocky Mountaineer use diesel). If an attendee took VIA Rail from elsewhere in Canada, their actual emissions are closer to 0.200 kg/km. We acknowledge this underestimates some rail journeys, but have chosen this factor to align with BC's cleaner electric transit infrastructure where possible.
How does the calculator know which train factor to use? It looks at the event city. Vancouver BC, Victoria BC, Kelowna BC → 0.008. Events outside BC in Canada → 0.200. US events → 0.071.

The 30 km rule

We used to classify anyone from BC, Washington, or Oregon as "already local." That was wrong — someone from Kelowna is 390 km from Vancouver, not local by any reasonable definition.


Now we use actual distance: under 30 km from the venue = local, no long-distance emissions counted. Over 30 km = they made a real trip.

V6B (Gastown) → ~1 km → local ✓
V6R (Kitsilano) → ~5 km → local ✓
V3A (Langley) → ~39 km → long-distance
V1Y (Kelowna) → ~390 km → long-distance
98101 (Seattle) → ~230 km → long-distance

Zip code lookup rules

1
Canadian postal codes — must start with a letter (e.g. V6B 2W9). We use the first 3 characters (the FSA: V6B) to look up coordinates. Spaces are stripped automatically.
2
US zip codes — must be exactly 5 digits (e.g. 98101). We do not pad shorter numbers. A 4-digit entry like 5778 is ignored — it could be Australian, South African, or a typo, and guessing would put someone in the wrong place.
3
Unrecognised or missing zip — that attendee's travel answers still count toward mode percentage breakdowns, but we can't calculate their actual distance. Instead, we use the fallback: 50 km for long-distance travel and 8 km for local commute. They are not excluded from the CO2 total.
This means attendees who left the zip blank still contribute to the average — just with less precision than those who provided one.

How we scale from respondents to all attendees

Not every attendee answers every question. We count anyone who answered at least one travel question (long-distance or local mode) as a respondent.

avg CO2 per respondent = (sum of all respondents' individual CO2) ÷ respondent count
total travel CO2 = avg CO2 per respondent × headcount
Important: "respondent" means anyone who answered a travel question — zip code or not. Those without a recognisable zip still contribute to the average using fallback distances (50 km / 8 km). Those who left both travel questions blank are skipped entirely.

Where does "headcount" come from?

Headcount is our best estimate of who actually showed up — not just who registered. We determine it in order of reliability:

  1. checked_in_at column present in CSV — we count only rows where checked_in_at has a timestamp. Blank = not checked in = excluded from headcount and from respondent counts. This is the most accurate source.
  2. checked_in_at column not present — all CSV rows are treated as attended (registrant list, no check-in data). The event organiser manually enters the total attendee count, and we use that for scaling.
Why this matters: Luma exports include everyone who registered, but only checked-in people actually traveled to the event. Using registrant count would overstate emissions. The checked_in_at column is the ground truth.

Example: 150 registered, 120 checked in. The CSV has 90 checked-in rows with travel answers. We calculate CO2 for those 90, find the average, then multiply by 120 (not 150).

⚡ Energy

What we're measuring

Electricity used to power the venue — lights, AV, HVAC, etc. We estimate consumption from venue size and event duration, then multiply by the grid emission factor for that location.

The formula

estimated kWh = (venue sq ft × 2 W/sq ft) ÷ 1000 × duration hours
energy CO2e = estimated kWh × grid emission factor

The 2 W/sq ft assumption is a typical lighting + AV load for a meeting/conference space. It's a rough average — not accounting for HVAC-heavy or server-room spaces.

Example: 2,000 sq ft venue, 4 hours, WA/OR grid.
kWh = (2,000 × 2) ÷ 1,000 × 4 = 16 kWh
CO2e = 16 × 0.288 = 4.6 kg

🥗 Catering

What we're measuring

Food production emissions — the carbon cost of growing, processing, and transporting the food served. We don't track transport to venue or packaging separately; those are embedded in the per-meal factors.

If no catering was provided, this category is zero. Rescue or surplus food (e.g. Too Good To Go, donated surplus) gets a near-zero factor because the production emissions are attributed to the original supply chain, not the event.

The formula

catering CO2e = portions served × emission factor (kg CO2e per portion)

Portions ≠ headcount. If an event serves breakfast and lunch to 50 people, that's 100 portions. Enter the total food units served, not the number of people.

🗑️ Waste

What we're measuring

Emissions from waste disposal. Landfill waste produces methane as it decomposes — high carbon impact. Recycling avoids some production emissions. Compost is carbon-negative because organic matter is diverted from landfill and returns nutrients to soil.

Two input methods

1
Bag counts (default) — Count the full bags after the event. A standard 30-gallon garbage bag is assumed at 12 kg. Enter counts per stream.
waste CO2e = (landfill bags × 12 × 0.639) + (recycling bags × 12 × 0.099) + (compost bags × 12 × −0.180)
Compost bags are carbon-negative — they reduce your total. Recycling has a small positive value because sorting and processing still uses energy.
2
Venue diversion report (override) — If the venue provides a waste report with total weight and diversion rate, use those instead. More accurate than bag counting.
landfill kg = total waste kg × (1 − diversion rate)
diverted kg = total waste kg × diversion rate
waste CO2e = (landfill kg × 0.639) + (diverted kg × −0.041)
Diverted average (−0.041) is the midpoint of recycling (0.099) and compost (−0.180), since the report doesn't break down stream composition.

📦 Materials

What we're measuring

Paper printed for the event — programmes, name badges, handouts, signage. Swag and signage type are collected for organisational records, but are not included in the CO2 number (too variable to estimate fairly).

The formula

materials CO2e = pages printed × 0.005 kg CO2e per page

0.005 kg/page accounts for paper production and printing energy. It assumes standard office paper (80 g/m², A4/Letter) and average grid electricity for printing.

Example: 200 pages printed.
CO2e = 200 × 0.005 = 1.0 kg — intentionally a small number. Paper matters at scale, not for a 200-page event pack.
🌟 Rating

How the rating is calculated

Once all five categories are summed, total CO2e is divided by total attendee headcount. That per-person figure determines the event's sustainability rating.

per-person CO2e = total CO2e ÷ attendee headcount

📊 The Dashboard

What's in the dashboard

Password-protected at /dashboard.html — team access only. Organisers see their own CO2 breakdown after submitting but never see the rating label or other events' data.

1
Summary stats — total events, total attendees, total CO2e, and average CO2e per person across all events.
2
Average CO2e by category — bar chart breaking down where emissions come from (travel, energy, catering, waste, materials).
3
By city — events, attendees, avg/person, and total CO2e grouped by city.
4
Ratings distribution — how many events fall in each rating tier.
5
Travel distance by event — for Luma CSV events: avg long-distance and local trip distances, attendee region breakdown (BC / PNW / national / Canada), and a "Pull from Luma" button to refresh live.
6
All events table — full list with date, city, attendees, per-person CO2e, total CO2e, rating badge, and submitter name. Edit button on every row.
7
Needs attention — auto-flags events rated "Below average" or "Needs improvement," with the organiser's own notes on what they'd improve.

Editing an event after submission

The team can edit any event's travel data from the dashboard. This is useful when an organiser submitted rough estimates and better data (like a Luma CSV) becomes available later.

1
Click Edit on any event row. A modal opens with the current travel mode splits and distances pre-filled.
2
Optionally upload a Luma CSV — this auto-fills all travel percentages, calculates per-attendee CO2 using actual zip distances, and auto-fills the checked-in headcount if the checked_in_at column is present.
3
CO2e totals and the rating update live as you adjust values. Save writes back to the database and the dashboard reflects the change immediately.
Export CSV — the dashboard has a one-click export of all event data as a spreadsheet, including every CO2 category, rating, submitter details, and improvement notes. Useful for reporting to funders or Climate Week partners.
